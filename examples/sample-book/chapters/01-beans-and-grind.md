# Chapter 1 - Beans and grind

## In one sentence

Good coffee starts before the water: fresh beans, ground just before brewing, to a size that fits the way you brew.

## How it works

1. **Beans.** Coffee is a seed. Light roasts keep more of the fruit and acid of the bean, dark roasts taste of the roast itself. Whole beans stay fresh for weeks after roasting; ground coffee goes flat in minutes.
2. **Grinding.** Grinding opens the bean so water can reach what is inside. A burr grinder makes pieces of an even size; a blade grinder smashes them into dust and boulders, and the dust turns bitter while the boulders stay sour.
3. **Size.** Fine for espresso, medium for a paper filter, coarse for a press. The finer the grind, the slower the water passes and the more it takes from the coffee.

## What goes wrong, and what people do about it

- **Stale beans** give a flat cup however well you brew. Buy small bags and use them within a month of the roast date.
- **Uneven grind** gives a cup that is sour and bitter at once. A burr grinder fixes it.
- **Very coarse for the method** gives a thin, watery cup.

## In the interview

**Q:** A customer says their coffee tastes both sour and bitter. What do you tell them?
**A:** That points to an uneven grind: the large pieces are under-extracted (sour) while the dust is over-extracted (bitter). I suggest a burr grinder, and then adjusting the size one step at a time.
